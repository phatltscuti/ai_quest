1. What is Google Pics?

Google Pics is Google Workspace's new AI image creation and editing tool, generally available since September 1, 2026. According to Workspace Updates, Pics brings “advanced AI image generation and precise, object-based image editing directly into your Workspace creative workflow”.

The key to understanding Pics: the official video description says it is “Built on our Nano Banana image generation and editing model”, and the Help Center says “Google Pics is an image creation and editing tool powered by Gemini (Nano Banana)”. In other words, NanoBanana is the engine (the model) and Google Pics is the car (the product/canvas) designed for visual work inside Workspace.

Quick facts (from official sources):
  • Access: pics.new (redirects to docs.google.com/images/create), or from Google Slides, Docs and Drive.
  • Devices: currently Desktop only (latest two versions of Chrome, Firefox, Edge).
  • Plans: Business Standard/Plus, Enterprise Standard/Plus, AI Expanded Access, Google AI Pro for Education, Google AI Pro, Google AI Ultra.
  • Admin: ON by default, can be disabled per domain/OU/group.
  • Prompt languages: 29 languages including Vietnamese.
  • Limits: “At least through February 28, 2027, users will have higher access” — access may be limited afterwards.

3. Google Pics vs NanoBanana: what is different?

NanoBanana is the name of Gemini's image model family: first Gemini 2.5 Flash Image (Aug 2025), then Nano Banana Pro (Gemini 3 Pro Image) and Nano Banana 2 (Gemini 3.1 Flash Image). People usually reach it through the Gemini app (chat → “Create image”), the Gemini API / AI Studio / Vertex AI, or image generation in Slides/Docs. Google Pics uses NanoBanana under the hood and adds a canvas built for design work.

In one sentence: Pics tackles “prompt-and-pray” — instead of rewriting the prompt ten times, you point at exactly what to change. NanoBanana is the brain; Pics is the workbench.
About the model version: official docs only say “Nano Banana”; some news sites (e.g. Camera Jabber) say Pics runs Nano Banana 2. Google does not state a version in the Help Center, so I do not claim one. Also note: in Slides/Docs, Pics usage “follow existing usage limits for Slides and Docs for Nano Banana”.

pics.new immediately creates a new Pics file (stored in Drive, URL docs.google.com/images/d/…). Minimal UI: a drop zone (Upload / Drive / Photos), the prompt bar “Bring your ideas to life with Gemini” with + (reference images) and Style; the top bar has Undo/Redo and a default 16:9 frame.

A 16:9 tech banner for "SCUTI AI - AI QUEST 2026". Bold headline "SCUTI AI", subtitle "Build. Learn. Ship with AI.". A cute red dragon mascot beside young Vietnamese engineers with laptops, glowing AI chat bubbles. Night city skyline, navy and red palette, flat vector style, footer "scuti.asia".

After Submit, about 15 seconds later Pics returns 4 variations in the left panel, each with a different layout. Click a thumbnail to preview, ✓ Confirm to keep it, or ↻ Generate again. In the chosen version “SCUTI AI”, “AI QUEST 2026” and “scuti.asia” are all spelled correctly. Pics also auto-named the file “Scuti AI Quest Banner”.

After Confirm, Pics runs “Detecting objects…”. Clicking the dragon outlines it and opens “Describe changes for the cartoon dragon” — Pics names the object itself. Type the change → Add → the bottom bar shows Discard / Apply 1 edit (several edits can be queued and applied together).

Step 16: Translate all text into Vietnamese with one prompt

Translate all text in this banner into Vietnamese. Keep "SCUTI AI" and "scuti.asia" unchanged. Keep the same fonts, colors and layout.

Result: “Xây dựng. Học hỏi. Ra mắt với AI.” — Vietnamese diacritics render correctly in the same font and position; “SCUTI AI” and “scuti.asia” are kept as asked. Interesting: Pics also translated the event name “AI QUEST 2026” into “CHINH PHỤC AI 2026” — list every proper noun you want to keep. The translation turn also returned 4 variations.

Step 17: Transform — frame ratio / rotation

Click the ratio button (16:9) → Transform – Adjust your frame with 16:9, 4:3, 3:2, 5:4, 1:1 (⋮ for more), a -90°…+90° rotation dial, zoom, Reset and Exit. The screenshot shows a 1:1 frame (for social posts); in the demo I switched back to 16:9 and exited to keep the banner.

Step 18: Export — Original / 2K / 4K

The Export icon (top right) offers Original size (1365×768), 2K JPEG and 4K JPEG (“Upscale with Gemini”). The 2K download took about 15 seconds; the actual file is 2752×1536 (~0.5 MB).

In gemini.google.com with the same account, send Create an image: <Step 13 prompt>. After about 25 seconds Gemini returns one image in the chat. 7. My opinion

What I like

  • It hits a real pain point: with NanoBanana in chat, fixing one small detail often changes something else. Click-to-select plus batching 5 edits is how designers work, not how chatbots work.
  • Text becomes an object: editing/translating text is what office teams need most (posters, cards, multilingual banners).
  • It lives in Workspace: sharing permissions, version history and admin control make it company-friendly compared with images scattered in personal chat history.
  • Slides “Replace image”: removes the download → edit → re-upload loop.

What to watch out for

  • Access barrier: Business Standard+ or AI Pro/Ultra; Business Starter is not included. Signed out, pics.new goes straight to the Sign in page.
  • Desktop only and still rolling out — eligible accounts may not see it yet.
  • Limits: “higher access” is only promised through Feb 28, 2027; 2K/4K downloads use limits faster.
  • Not an API replacement: for hundreds of data-driven images, use NanoBanana via the Gemini API.
  • The official video says “Sequences shortened and simulated” — yet in my real run each turn took only ~15 seconds, faster than expected.
  • Over-eager translation: Pics translated the event name “AI QUEST” too — list every proper noun that must stay unchanged.

9. Prompting tips for Pics

  • Be specific: subject, action, setting, mood. “A cat” → “A fluffy ginger cat napping on a sun-drenched windowsill”.
  • Positive framing: “empty street” instead of “no cars”.
  • Specify style & lighting: “flat vector illustration”, “three-point softbox setup”, “golden hour backlighting”.
  • Put text to render in double quotes and keep it short.
  • Iterate: start simple, then edit elements instead of rewriting everything.
  • Press ↑ in the prompt bar to reuse past prompts (seen in the official video).

10. Conclusion

Google Pics is not a new model — it is NanoBanana packaged as a design tool inside Workspace: variations, element-level edits, text editing/translation, crop, 2K/4K, version history, sharing and Slides/Docs/Drive integration. If you need precise edits and teamwork in Workspace, use Pics. If you need automation and scale, use NanoBanana through the API.
